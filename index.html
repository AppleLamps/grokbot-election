The bottom line

If the polls are roughly right, Democrats are favored to win both chambers: about a 68% chance of a Senate majority and 93% for the House, riding a generic-ballot lead of D+8.4. Republicans' realistic path to holding both chambers requires the polls to be understating Republicans, as they did in 2016, 2020 and 2024. In that scenario, Republicans become Senate favorites (about 65%), but Democrats still win the House roughly 70% of the time. If polls instead miss in Democrats' favor, as in the 2025 off-year elections, it becomes a Democratic wave in both chambers.

Pick a polling-error scenario

Polls are never perfect, and their errors tend to point the same way in every race. Each scenario shifts that shared, nationwide error.

Why +3 one way and +6 the other?

Each scenario copies the size of a real, recent polling miss rather than mirroring the other. In presidential years polls overstated Democrats by about 3 points (2016: 3.0, 2020: 4.7, 2024: 2.9). In the 2025 off-year elections they overstated Republicans by about 6. Recent midterms were close to even (2018: R+0.5, 2022: D+0.8), which is the “unbiased” case. Every scenario also keeps a ±3-point standard deviation around its average miss.

How the model works

  1. Estimate each race. Senate: a weighted blend of the race's polling average and a fundamentals estimate (Cook PVI × 2 + the D+8.4 generic ballot + incumbency + candidate adjustments). House: fundamentals only (PVI + generic ballot + incumbency), across all 435 districts on the 2026 maps.
  2. Add correlated polling error. In every simulation, one national polling-error term shifts every race the same direction, on top of independent race-level noise. That is why polling misses tend to sweep the board instead of cancelling out.
  3. Simulate 100,000 elections per scenario and count who controls each chamber, including how independents caucus.
  4. Vary the polling-error assumption: centered at zero, a 3-point pro-Democratic polling miss (the 2016/2020/2024 pattern), or a 6-point pro-Republican miss (the 2025 pattern).

!Honest limitations

  • Snapshot as of October 10, 2026. Polls, candidates and ratings change; nothing here updates automatically.
  • The House model is crude. It uses only partisan lean, the generic ballot and incumbency, with no district polls, candidate quality or fundraising. It likely over-credits the Democratic wave by roughly 10 seats.
  • Simplified, not a professional forecast. Scenario parameters are judgment calls, and several Senate races have little or no public polling.
  • The scenarios are “what if” lenses, not predictions of which polling miss will happen.
